A deep reinforcement-learning agent trades a simulated battery on Australia's electricity market. How much of the profit available with hindsight can it capture while seeing only what was known at the time? Forward test: 1 September to 6 October 2026, five regions. It has since stopped.

<RbitCharts part="charts" />

The price axis is clipped at the 99th percentile; hover values are unclipped. Times are NEM time (AEST).

## Results

<RbitCharts part="summary" />

The agent captured 33% (TAS1) to 64% (QLD1) of perfect-foresight profit, cycling about once a day. Net profit is arbitrage revenue minus degradation; perfect foresight is the most the same battery could earn knowing every price in advance.

## Method

Every five minutes the agent picks charge, hold or discharge at full power (dueling double DQN, noisy-network exploration). It sees only data published before that interval: price, demand and interconnector lags, AEMO's 5- and 30-minute forecasts, and its state of charge. The battery has 90% round-trip efficiency and a $10/MWh degradation cost; it is a price taker in the energy market only. The benchmark is a linear program over the whole period. Data: AEMO's NEMweb.

## Limitations

- 36 days in one season; other conditions could look quite different.
- The benchmark must end with an empty battery, which inflates "% of perfect" slightly. I did not measure by how much.
- Flat degradation cost, no network charges, no frequency-control revenue, no price impact. Simulated dispatch, not a real battery.
- The daily job has stopped, so these numbers are a fixed snapshot.
