A grid battery makes money by charging when wholesale electricity is cheap and discharging when it is expensive. In Australia's National Electricity Market (NEM) the price is set every five minutes and swings from negative to thousands of dollars per MWh, so the question is how much of the available profit a model can capture when it only sees what was known at the time.

I trained a deep Q-network agent to make that decision for a simulated battery, then ran it forward every day from 1 September 2026 on new market data, in all five mainland regions. The forward test ran until 6 October 2026 and then stopped. This page is the final result.

## Results

36 days, 1 September to 6 October 2026. Net profit is arbitrage revenue minus degradation cost. "Perfect foresight" is the most the same battery could have earned if it had known every price in advance.

The agent captured between a third and two thirds of the perfect-foresight profit, depending on the region: 64% in QLD1 and 33% in TAS1. It cycled the battery about once a day (31 to 47 cycles in 36 days). Pick a region to see where the gap opens up.

## Method

- **Battery:** 200 MW / 400 MWh, 90% round-trip efficiency, $10/MWh degradation cost on every MWh charged or discharged. Price taker, energy market only.
- **Agent:** a dueling double DQN with noisy-network exploration. Every five minutes it chooses charge, hold or discharge at full power, with an action mask that rules out physically impossible moves.
- **Inputs:** lagged prices, demand and interconnector flows, plus AEMO's own 5-minute (P5) and 30-minute pre-dispatch forecasts, and the battery's state of charge. Each decision uses only reports published before the interval it applies to.
- **Benchmark:** a linear program over the whole period with the same battery and perfect knowledge of actual prices.

## Data sources

AEMO's NEMweb: dispatch prices, demand and interconnector flows; P5 and pre-dispatch forecast reports. A daily GitHub Actions job ingested new reports, computed features, ran the agent and stored the results.

## Limitations

- **Short test:** 36 days in one season. Results in other conditions could look quite different, and I have not tested that.
- **The benchmark is slightly handicapped:** the linear program must end the period with an empty battery, while the agent can hold charge. This inflates "% of perfect" a little; I did not measure by how much.
- **Simplified economics:** flat degradation cost, no network charges, no frequency-control (FCAS) revenue, and no effect of the battery's own bids on prices.
- **Not a trading result:** these are simulated dispatches priced at the published regional price, not a battery that was actually operated.
- **No longer running:** the daily job and the dashboard it fed have been retired; the numbers above are a fixed snapshot.
